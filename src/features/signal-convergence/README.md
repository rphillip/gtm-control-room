# Signal Convergence Playground

The page offers two explicit reading depths: a plain-English clue metaphor for readers without a data background and a concise engineering-proof summary for GTM hiring managers. Its red data ball gains reach as independent evidence converges, passes through a human-investigation gate, and loops back without implying a guaranteed purchase.

This interactive portfolio artifact teaches a practical GTM engineering idea: one signal may be noise, while different evidence streams agreeing can justify investigating an account. It grew from Ryan Sulapas's employer-account analysis for a hypothetical MSK healthcare GTM motion. It does not represent work for, an affiliation with, or outcomes from Hinge Health.

## Public-data boundary

The account, company attributes, evidence, and interaction are synthetic. No Clay rows, private company information, inferred facts about a real employer, workspace identifiers, credentials, or remote API calls are used. The authored examples live in `model.ts` and execute entirely in the browser.

## Model limitations

- The default 35 / 30 / 35 weights are illustrative educated guesses, not fitted or validated coefficients.
- The Priority Score orders investigation; it does not predict purchase intent or conversion.
- Correlated evidence streams should not be counted automatically as independent confirmation.
- Missing public evidence does not prove that a condition is absent.
- A production version should preserve raw evidence and compare signal combinations with downstream outcomes, then recalibrate or remove assumptions that do not hold up.

## Adapting the pattern

Replace the synthetic signal definitions with evidence relevant to the business change you want to detect. Normalize every source to a shared account identity, retain provenance, route convergent accounts to human review, record outcomes, and make the feedback loop part of the system. The pattern is independent of any one tool: Clay, a CRM, warehouse/dbt models, APIs, enrichment providers, telemetry, and intent sources can all supply evidence.
